# Letter to HUMAX UK support — information request for CXHD-5150C

**To:** uksupport@humax-digital.co.uk
**Subject:** Information request — Humax CXHD-5150C cable receiver (OnLime/Rostelecom, Russia)

---

Hello,

I am writing to ask about a Humax CXHD-5150C DVB-C receiver — the operator
variant that was distributed in Russia around 2011–2012 by OnLime, which was
later absorbed into Rostelecom/Mostelecom.

A bit of background about me: I am a hardware enthusiast from Russia, and
together with a small community of like-minded people I try to give old
set-top boxes a second life instead of letting them become e-waste — very
much in the spirit of what the hummy.tv community has done for the
HDR-FOX T2 / HD-FOX T2 family over the years. The CXHD-5150C is a lovely,
well-built BCM7405-based machine, and there are plenty of them still around
in perfect working order. The operator services they were tied to, however,
were shut down long ago, so today these boxes are effectively bricked by
their operator firmware.

First of all — thank you for keeping your open-source portal alive. The
published kernel configuration for the CXHD-5150C and the GCC 4.2 / uClibc
crosstools archive for Linux 2.6.18 have already been enormously helpful
for my project.

My device, for reference:
  Model:     Humax CXHD-5150C (loader version Y5.06)
  System ID: 8050.8400, Market ID 0x83
  MAC:       08:EB:74:8C:C8:C0 (your OUI)
  Serial:    13110834507683

I would be extremely grateful for anything you are able to share about this
model beyond what is already on your website, for example:

  - any firmware update file (.hdf) for the CXHD-5150C, or for its close
    relatives (CXHD-5000C / CXHD-5100C / HD 7000i), in any version;
  - technical or service documentation: bootloader behaviour, flash memory
    map, update file format;
  - even a pointer to where such materials could officially be obtained.

I fully understand that this was an operator-specific product, that some of
the materials may belong to the operator rather than to Humax, and that
company policy may simply not allow you to help. A short "unfortunately we
cannot assist with this" would be a perfectly acceptable answer, and I will
be grateful for any reply at all.

Thank you very much for your time, and for building hardware that is still
worth saving fourteen years later.
